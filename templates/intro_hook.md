# Intro Hook Template

Use as starting structure for U01 hook unit. Customize per film.

Two variants supported based on channel positioning + platform:

## Variant A: Atmospheric (Restrained Register A)

Use for: documentary-style channels, prestige positioning, YouTube long-form
audiences who watch full videos start to finish.

### Structure (60-80 words total)

**[ATMOSPHERIC SETUP] (~20 words):**
Open with location + situation. Specific, sensory, no exposition.

**[TENSION/MYSTERY] (~20-25 words):**
Foreshadow the central conflict or mystery WITHOUT spoiler.
Abstract phrasing: "the truth was hiding closer than anyone could imagine"

**[CHANNEL + FILM TITLE] (~15-20 words):**
"I'm [channel name], and today we'll be reviewing [Film Title (Year)]"

**[THEMATIC TEASER] (~15-20 words):**
One sentence about film's central question or theme.

### Example (Prisoners 2013 - Atmospheric):

> "A quiet Pennsylvania town. A Thanksgiving meal between two families.
> And then two little girls walked out the front door and never came back.
> The case would push one detective to his limits, and one father across
> every line. But the truth was hiding closer than anyone could imagine.
> I'm Serabudabu, and today we'll be reviewing Prisoners (2013) - Denis
> Villeneuve's psychological thriller that asks how much a person will
> sacrifice to find what they've lost."

## Variant B: Viral (Commercial Register B - default for film recap channels)

Use for: YouTube algorithm-optimized recap channels, TikTok-cross-posted
content, audiences with high drop-off in first 3 seconds.

Core principle: First 3 seconds must hook viewer to prevent algorithmic drop-off.
Visual + verbal both engaging immediately.

### Structure (60-80 words total)

**[INTRIGUE COLD OPEN] (~15-20 words):**
Drop viewer into peak tension moment. NO atmospheric build. Either:
- Quick teaser of climactic scene (e.g., bloody return, gun reveal, scream)
- Provocative question that demands answer
- Specific, visceral image stated

Bind this to peak intrigue footage from later in film (NOT atmospheric early
scenes). Use narrative_jump=true. Avoid spoiling the twist or ending - tease
without revealing.

**[STAKES + QUESTION] (~20-25 words):**
Establish what's at stake + the question recap will answer. Direct viewer
address acceptable.

Examples:
- "What would you do if your wife disappeared on your anniversary?"
- "How well do you really know the person sleeping next to you?"
- "When the police can't find your daughter, how far would you go?"

**[CHANNEL + FILM TITLE] (~15-20 words):**
"I'm [channel name], and today we're diving into [Film Title (Year)]"

Note: "diving into" / "going inside" feels more engaging than "reviewing."

**[PROMISE/TEASE] (~15-20 words):**
What makes this film worth watching the recap for. The promise of the
journey ahead.

Examples:
- "...one of the most disturbing thrillers ever made"
- "...where every clue is a lie, and the truth comes at a cost"
- "...a film that asks how much a person will sacrifice to find what they've lost"

### Example (Gone Girl 2014 - Viral):

> "On the morning of their fifth anniversary, Amy Dunne walked out of her
> house and never came back. The investigation that follows will tear her
> husband's life apart - and the truth, when it comes, will be worse than
> anyone could imagine. How well do you really know the person sleeping
> next to you? I'm Serabudabu, and today we're diving into Gone Girl (2014)
> - a psychological thriller where every clue is a lie, and the truth comes
> at a cost."

(Cold open binds to the bloody-return shot, B31 area ~02:06:15 - teases
intrigue without revealing the twist.)

### Example (Prisoners 2013 - Viral):

> "Two little girls walked out the front door and never came back. The
> only suspect just walked free. And one father is about to cross every
> line.
> How far would you go to bring your daughter home?
> I'm Serabudabu, and today we're diving into Prisoners (2013) - a thriller
> that turns one man's love into a question he cannot answer."

## Visual Binding Rules

### Variant A (Atmospheric):
Bind to early-film atmospheric setup shots only:
- Establishing shots (location, weather, time)
- Character intro shots (no key revelations)
- Mood-setting moments

AVOID: climax shots, action peaks, emotional climaxes, twist reveal shots

### Variant B (Viral):
Mix of footage types allowed:
- ONE peak intrigue shot in cold open (teases tension, doesn't spoil)
- Atmospheric establishing shots in middle of hook
- Title/channel intro over neutral visual

Specific binding cautions for Variant B:
- Cold open peak shot should be SHORT (2-3 seconds visible)
- Cannot reveal twist (e.g., for Gone Girl, can show Amy bloody return but
  NOT Amy driving alive)
- Cannot show outcome (e.g., no shooting deaths visible in hook)
- Tease without resolving

## Selecting Variant per Film

Default for commercial film recap channels: **Variant B (Viral)**

Use Variant A only when:
- Channel positioning is prestige/documentary
- Film tone is meditative/slow-burn (doesn't have peak intrigue moments
  worth teasing)
- Audience known to prefer literary opening

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

Even in Variant B Viral, avoid these patterns:

### Clickbait that lies
BAD: "You won't BELIEVE what happens next..."
WHY: Generic, no specific content, feels deceptive

### Aggregator phrasing
BAD: "In this video, we're going to talk about..."
WHY: Boring, generic YouTube template feel

### Spoiler in hook
BAD: "Amy faked her own death and framed her husband"
WHY: Removes reason to watch recap

### Over-promise
BAD: "The most shocking thriller of all time"
WHY: Sets bar that may not deliver, viewer disappointed

### Generic stakes
BAD: "A man's life will never be the same"
WHY: Vague, every story has stakes, doesn't specify

Note: An earlier draft of this template contained a Gone Girl example that
revealed the twist ("Amy is alive..."). That example was inconsistent with the
template's own anti-spoiler rules and has been corrected. Twist discipline
applies to hooks too - tease without revealing reveals.

## Hook Quality Checklist

Before approving hook draft:

- [ ] First 3 seconds verbally hook (specific image OR provocative question)
- [ ] First visual is engaging (peak intrigue or strong establishing)
- [ ] No twist/ending spoiled
- [ ] Specific to THIS film (not generic thriller template)
- [ ] Channel branding integrated naturally
- [ ] Promise/tease specific enough to attract right audience
- [ ] Tone matches recap body that follows (no whiplash)
