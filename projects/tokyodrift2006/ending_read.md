## Findings

**Line 110.** "He has it coming" is the wrong register for the sentence it sits in. Everything around it is affectionate about Twinkie — he funded the kid, he is thriving, he is working a crowd — and then the closing clause uses an idiom that in ordinary English means a comeuppance is deserved. On one hearing it reads as the recap turning on a character it had just been kind to. If the intent was "he has earned it," it does not survive being heard once.

Also in 110: "the kid" is used as a known quantity before anyone has been named. Sean does not appear until 111, so for the duration of the first line the kid is a stranger. And "he called the kid crazy and funded him anyway" is a callback with no anchor — I am told a thing was said, but not when, not where, and not in what scene, so it feels summarised rather than quoted.

**Line 111.** This is the hardest line to follow. "The smirk drops" has no stated owner — Twinkie has been the smirking showman for a line and a half, but Sean has just been called out, so either face is plausible. Then "the woman in red holds his eye while he takes that in" gives me two men and two unanchored pronouns in one clause; I had to stop and work out that it is probably Sean, and by your standard that counts. The woman in red herself is introduced as though I already know her — she is given a costume instead of a name, here and again in 113, and she never gets one. And the pivot of the whole line, "somebody who told Twinkie that Han was family," is a callback to a conversation I was never in: who said it, to whom, where, when.

**Line 112.** "A challenger rolls up beside him" inherits the pronoun fog from 111 — "him" has to be Sean, but nothing in the sentence makes that so. "You know this ain't no ten-second race" is delivered as a line with history, and the phrase is clearly meant to mean something, but nothing in these five lines tells me what a ten-second race is, so the warning lands as sound rather than threat. And the closing sentence — "the last man who pulled alongside him like this is the reason he can drift at all" — is the clearest unlanded callback in the set: the man is never named, the scene is never placed, and the sentence contains two more "him/he" that I had to assign by guesswork.

**Line 113.** "That rooftop" is the plainest unintroduced referent here; the demonstrative insists I was shown a rooftop scene, and within these lines I was not. More seriously, the quoted line itself is in the third person: "on the line Han gave him on that rooftop and never explained: he doesn't drift to win." If Han said it to Sean, the words should be Han's, and as written I cannot tell whether "he" is Han describing himself or Han describing Sean — which means I cannot tell who is speaking in the moment the recap has chosen as its last image. "And never explained" then tells me outright that the idea the ending rests on was left unresolved, which reads less like restraint than like a dangling thread.

**Lines 113 and 114 together.** 113 says "that's where it ends," and then 114 happens. One of those two is the ending; having both makes the close stutter.

**Line 114.** "Sean never did get a car of his own" is delivered as the payoff to a running thread, but within these lines no such want was ever stated, so the sentence resolves something I was not shown wanting. It is also sitting immediately after a line in which he is plainly at the wheel of something, which made me re-read it.
