# Story guide: how this channel tells a film

Replaces `recap_style.md` and `NARRATOR_VOICE.md` (now in `docs/archive/`).
Owner-approved on 17 Nov 2026. The model is a hand-written act 3 of *Furious 7*,
which the owner read and called right: "this is how it should sound, not the
old metaphors nobody can follow".

## 0. The job

Someone who has never seen the film listens **once**, without looking at the
screen, and can tell the story back: who wanted what, who did what to whom,
and how it ended. A friend who has just watched the film is telling it to you,
clearly, with a reaction now and then.

Hooks, rhythm and voice matter only on top of that. A recap that is lively but
cannot be followed has failed. One that is plain and clear has not.

## 1. Understand the film before writing a line (angle and plan)

- **The spine is a chain of causes.** Each step happens *because* of an
  earlier one: "Shaw wants Dom dead, Jakande wants God's Eye, so they team up."
  If you cannot say why a step happens, you do not understand the film yet.
- **Track who holds what.** Key objects, alliances and secrets, and the moment
  each changes hands: God's Eye goes from the crew to Jakande to Ramsey. A
  viewer who loses track of these loses the plot.
- **Write three questions** a listener must be able to answer at the end
  ("Why are Shaw and Jakande on the same side?"). The recap is written so
  those three are answered without effort.

## 2. One line

- **Who does what, and why or with what result.** "Ramsey switches God's Eye
  on. It breaks into every camera at Etihad Towers and starts scanning faces."
- **Names.** The first time someone appears, give their name and their role:
  "Kamata, the yakuza boss whose turf this all runs on." After that use the
  name. Use "he" or "she" only when no one else in the scene could be meant.
  A name that sounds like an ordinary word is always said in full: "Mr.
  Nobody", never a bare "Nobody".
- **Cause words, not time words.** Use "so", "because", "but", "to find him",
  "it turns out". Avoid "later", "meanwhile", "by now", which only say that
  time passed.
- **Plain words.** No metaphor the listener has to decode, no riddle, no
  aphorism. If a line needs a second hearing, it is wrong.
- **What happens, not how it was filmed.** No camera or editing words (camera,
  cut, shot, frame, close-up, on-screen). The viewer is already watching.
- **One line = one thing on screen.** A line names only as many visible events
  as its footage shows. Background goes in background grammar: "the car he has
  just borrowed, the one he will wreck", not "he borrows it, wrecks it and owes
  for it".

BAD -> GOOD, from our own past drafts:
- "Mr. Nobody, who took a bullet meant for all of them, still holds the leash
  on God's Eye." (wrong, and a riddle)
  -> "In the chaos, Jakande's men take the laptop with God's Eye on it."
- "It is actually a ledger - every turn in this story is a payment." (decode me)
  -> "Her family marries her off to a stranger to pay one hospital bill."
- "By then, Hobbs walks in." (the climax happened off the page)
  -> "Dom drives his Charger up the crumbling garage, launches it at the
  helicopter, and throws a bag of grenades inside in mid-air."

## 3. Between scenes

- When you skip ahead, the next line carries the reason: "So the crew goes home
  to Los Angeles, to fight on streets they know." "To find Shaw, Dom calls Mr.
  Nobody."
- When an object, an alliance or a secret changes hands, say so in one plain
  sentence. Never let it happen between two lines.
- Never refer to something as already known ("the deal", "that promise", "his
  partner") unless an earlier line introduced it.

## 4. Dialogue

- **Say what was said.** Paraphrase it: "Mr. Nobody wants to wait for dawn. Dom
  says no: they go now." Never "they argue" or "he explains" when you know the
  content. Never "whatever he says" or "says something".
- **Quote only a line that is itself the payoff**: "The thing about street
  fights: the street always wins."
- The transcript says what is said and who says it. It does not say what
  happens on screen. If the transcript and the picture disagree, the picture
  wins. If a beat names two people and does not say who speaks, check the
  transcript before you give the line to one of them.
- **Let the film speak** two or three times: a line of real dialogue played
  without narration, framed so the listener knows who is speaking and why it
  matters.

## 5. Action

- How much to narrate depends on the scene. Some fights need four lines;
  some need one.
- What must always be said is the **outcome that changes the story**: who is
  hurt, who is caught, who escapes, who now holds what. "Mr. Nobody's men are
  cut down one by one, and Mr. Nobody himself is shot."
- Spectacle with no change in it (a stunt, a chase that ends where it began)
  can play on picture and music.

## 6. Your reactions

- A reaction is what a friend watching with you would say: a joke, surprise, or
  being moved. It is concrete and understood on one hearing. "That is not how
  casts work. But it is how Hobbs works." "Which, to be fair, is a great reason
  to stay in your cell."
- Use one when it helps, not on a schedule. Keep it to about one line in five
  at most. There is no minimum.
- No lines that open "That's the ..." or "Here's the ...". No phrases you made
  up and then repeat as a refrain ("the ledger", "the exchange rate"). No stock
  moves repeated across the video.
- At a big moment (a death, a win, a reveal), the moment comes first, in plain
  words or with the film's own line. Then at most one reaction.

## 7. Emotional moments: depth

Depth means saying **what this moment means to the character**, using facts
from the film: what they have lost, how long they have waited, what it cost
them to get here. It does not come from a metaphor or a big word.

- Flat: "Letty remembers, and Dom wakes up."
- Too clever: "The cost of the film, said by the one who forgot, is what
  stings."
- Deep: "For two movies she couldn't remember marrying him. The memory comes
  back at the exact moment she's about to lose him."

Give an emotional moment room: one or two lines that set up what is at stake,
then the moment, then, at most, the line that says what it means. Do not
explain the feeling ("it is heartbreaking"). State the facts that make it
heartbreaking.

## 8. Opening, intro, ending

- **Opening:** start on the film's own question, the situation that makes
  someone want to know what happens. The question must be specific to this
  film. The open is planned in code (cold open, genre lever), so follow the
  slots you are given.
- **Intro:** the channel name, the film and its year, the director, and the
  two leads with their roles, in a sentence or two. Then straight into the
  story.
- **Ending:** say what the ending means in one or two plain lines, then the
  sign-off. Do not just report the last event.

## 9. Facts (each rule exists because a mistake shipped)

- **Do not invent.** Every event is in the beat map or the transcript.
- **Places and times come from the beats you are covering, not from memory.**
  A bridge line once put a race "on the mountain" when it happened in a car
  park.
- **A callback** ("the same move that got him shipped out") names the beat
  where that event happens (`callback_beat`).
- **A claim about habit or repetition** ("always", "kept", "every time", "the
  whole film") needs a beat or transcript line showing it more than once.
  Otherwise, say what happened once.
- **Spoilers:** keep a reveal back until the film reveals it. Once it lands,
  say it plainly and use the character's name.
- **Spell names** exactly as `names.json` does. Never hide an identity behind
  a description ("the greying man") once a name or a role is known.

## 10. Length and footage

- Each line's footage decides its maximum length. The slots say "up to N words".
  Use fewer when the story needs fewer. Never pad to fill a slot.
- The whole video has a runtime ceiling that code checks. Buy room for an
  important scene by cutting repetition and restatement elsewhere, never by
  writing more.
- A beat with nothing to say can be held on footage. Silence after a death, a
  reveal or a climax is allowed, sparingly.
