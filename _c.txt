motion: cap slow-mo at 1.15x, cap freezes, forbid reused footage (item 3)

Three ways the picture stopped being real footage, all previously allowed.

1. SLOW-MOTION 1.40x -> 1.15x (config.BINDING_SLOWDOWN_MAX, which schemas now
   reads instead of holding its own copy). The stretch is `setpts` - frame
   duplication with no interpolation - so past roughly 1.15x it reads as
   judder, not as a slow-motion effect.

2. FREEZE. `tpad=stop_mode=clone` had stop_duration set to the whole clip
   length, i.e. NO CEILING: a short window under a long line became a still
   image for seconds. All four tpad sites are capped at config.MAX_FREEZE_SEC
   (0.30s). FREEZE-CAPPED asserts it against render.py's source, so a new
   uncapped tpad fails in the test rather than in a render.

3. REUSE. Nothing stopped two lines binding overlapping footage.

MULTI-SHOT EXTENSION is the other half of lowering the cap. tools/
fit_narration.py used to clamp a widened window to its host shot, on the
reasoning that crossing a cut changes the picture mid-line. At 1.15x that
reasoning inverts: the alternative to crossing a cut is stretching one shot,
and real footage with a real cut in it is better picture than the same shot
slowed down. A window now runs on across the following cuts, bounded by the
next BOUND LINE, by any reveal landing, and by the source duration.

POST-RENDER CHECK (pipeline/motion_check.py), fatal, on the finished file:
 - ffmpeg freezedetect: any frozen stretch over config.FREEZE_FAIL_SEC (0.50s)
   fails, with timestamps in the finished file's own clock. Stretches under it
   are still reported, so near-misses are visible instead of the report only
   ever saying pass or fail.
 - duplicate footage across lines, preferring what the render actually SHOWED
   over what the draft declared.
Prevention and detection are deliberately separate: a freeze can also come
from a static frame in the source, a filter graph that stalls, or a cached clip
built under older rules - none of which the build rules can see.
A cheap [FOOTAGE-REUSE] report also runs at draft time, before any encoding.

TWO REAL BUGS THE WORK EXPOSED:

 - _build_silence_clip IGNORES its `end` argument. It reads `-ss start -t
   clip_len`, so a hold whose authored duration exceeds its window keeps
   playing into the FOLLOWING shots. The render was recording [start, end) as
   the footage a hold shows, so the censor audit never looked at the footage a
   long hold actually reaches - flagged content just past a hold's window could
   play unblurred. Now recorded as [start, start+clip_len). REUSE-USES-SHOWN
   and HOLD-WINDOW-HONEST lock both halves.

 - THE INTRO MONTAGE WAS REUSING STORY FOOTAGE. pick_moments chooses from the
   whole catalogue, so it was picking shots that lines are also bound to: the
   same shot in the teaser and again in the body. Found by the new check
   failing two censor tests, which is the test suite doing its job. The montage
   is the element with free choice of material, so it is the one that moves -
   it now skips footage the story already shows and says how many it skipped.
   On tokyodrift2006 it skips 59 moments and still finds its 8.

Also fixed: recap.py's hard-audit message said "exceed 1.4x shot capacity" from
a hardcoded constant; it reads BINDING_SLOWDOWN_MAX now, so it cannot describe
a ceiling the pipeline no longer uses.
And tools/test_render_smoke.py now disables stem separation for its fixture -
its "film" is a test pattern with a sine tone, so there is no dialogue to
separate and Demucs would add minutes to a test about the render path.

tools/test_motion.py: 8 cases, all pass. Full suite green: gate_selftest 28,
quality 7, voice 10, preflight 6, render_smoke 5, angle_gates,
render_position.
